Minority voters seek descriptive representation to achieve substantive outcomes,
yet shared identity cannot guarantee the contextual knowledge group-specific policy
demands. I update the agency framework to introduce domain-specific competency
into the incumbent’s behavioral calculus, providing a general approach to group-based
substantive representation. I apply this dimension of accountability to racial dynamics
by introducing racial competence. Multiracial politicians serve as a unique case, as
their framing as descriptive representatives of multiple groups leaves their racial com-
petence in question. Results qualify the notion that a Multiracial identity provides
a strategic advantage: voters’ perceptions of racial competence drive incumbents to
posture, risking exposure when future periods demand contextual knowledge. Exten-
sions show how electoral and social institutions alter the incumbent’s behavior. Racial
gerrymandering amplifies posturing and lowers voter welfare, while caucus membership
provides insurance against future demands for competence. Domain-specific compe-
tency provides a framework for examining formal extensions and empirical implications
of Multiracial behavior.
