How do voters respond when Black women candidates express anger–particularly when that anger is racialized? Using a preregistered survey experiment (N ≈ 5,000) varying candidate race, gender, and emotional expression, we find that anger penalties for Black women are not universal but conditional.  Racially resentful White voters substantially penalize an angry Black woman candidate, reducing predicted vote likelihood by half the scale in the non-racialized (b=-0.51, p<.01) and by nearly one-third in the racialized anger conditions (b=-0.31, p<.10).  Even racially liberal White women, who initially reward non-racialized anger from the Black woman candidate, reduce their support once that anger is framed around racial injustice, revealing the fragility of cross-racial gender solidarity. Among Black respondents, community nationalists amplify support for the Black man candidate when he expresses racialized anger but provide no such reward for the Black woman candidate making an identical appeal.  In sum, anger is not a freely available political resource for Black women candidates.
